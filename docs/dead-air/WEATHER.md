# Weather: the Zone's climate (1.6.0)

The weather of the Exclusion Zone follows its climate. With **"Weather by the Zone's climate"** on (weather dialog ->
Seasons; console `r__season_weather`, on by default) the engine decides, hour by hour, what the sky does, how warm the
air is, what falls and how hard the wind blows, in the shares of the hours the Chornobyl records give each month; the
snowpack and the ice are what that weather left. The season day `D` (SEASONS.md) chooses the month. Off: AtmosFear's
own period graphs pick the sky as before, and the snow and the ice follow the season keys alone (WINTER.md).

Code: `src/xrEngine/da_climate.{h,cpp}` (the climate, the snowpack, the ice, the sky clock and the sun),
`src/xrEngine/da_ice.{h,cpp}` (the ice under a weight), `src/xrPhysics/ph_ice.{h,cpp}` (the collision),
`src/xrGame/da_ice_game.{h,cpp}` (cracks, holes, bullets, blasts), the weather manager's hook
`gamedata/scripts/dead_air_x64_season_weather.script`, the data `[weather_keys]` and `[climate]` of
`configs/dead_air_x64_seasons.ltx` (generated from `tools/seasons/data/weather_keys.json`) and `[ice]` of
`configs/dead_air_x64_water.ltx`, the cycles `configs/environment/weathers/af3_*_{clear_steady,rain_steady,snow,
blizzard}*.ltx` (generated by `tools/seasons/gen_weather_cycles.py`), the sounds `sounds/dead_air_x64/ice/*.ogg`
(`tools/ice/ice_sounds.py`).

## Everything is a function of time

The climate is a pure function of the absolute game time, the season day and the game's **climate seed**: nothing of
the weather is saved but the seed (`dead_air_x64_climate_seed` in the scripts' save state; a game saved before this
gets one at its next load). A reload replays the same weather, and the snowpack and the ice of a moment are the
integral of the weather of the last 150 game days, recomputed at a load (about 30 ms), a jump back in time, a seed
change or a season moved by more than a day; between those, every frame integrates on in steps of at most an hour.
The history walks the season backwards with the game days (`D - days ago`), so a February load finds a pond frozen
since December. A QA sweep of the season (`qa_season_year`, `qa_season_pin`) and a glide between two choices show
the season keys instead and leave the integration alone.

## The weather of an hour

Five smooth random processes of game time, each two octaves of value noise at unit variance, with their own scales
(`[climate] tau_*`, game days):

| Process | Scale | Couplings |
|---|---|---|
| temperature anomaly `zt` | 2.5 d | da_precip's synoptic anomaly (seed 0 keeps the earlier temperatures) |
| cloud `zc` | 1.3 d | `cloud_t_corr` x zt: a clear winter is a cold anticyclone, a clear summer a hot one |
| wetness `q` | 0.28 d | 0.6 zc + 0.8 of its own: rain and snow come out of the cloud |
| wind `zw` | 0.45 d | 0.45 q + 0.89 of its own: fronts are windy |
| fog `zf` | 0.6 d | plus the hour's weight: dawn in summer, all day in winter (`fog_dawn`) |

At the first use the engine calibrates thresholds per month by 4000 samples of those very processes, so that the month
gets the share of its hours `[weather_keys]` asks for: wet (`p_wet`), foggy among the dry calm hours (`p_fog`, wind
under `fog_wind_max`), overcast and clear among the rest (`p_overcast` counts the wet hours, `p_clear`), blizzard among
the snowy wet hours (`p_blizzard`), rainstorm among the rainy ones (`p_storm`); the rate base that gives the month its
precipitation (`precip_mm`). The log prints them (`* [climate] jan: wet z ...`).

The sky of an hour block (`da_climate_sample`, `EDaSky`):

1. **wet** (`q` over its threshold at the block's middle): the six-hour block's phase (snow, sleet, rain - the
   logistic of da_precip on the day's temperature with the climate's own `phase_bias` / `phase_sigma`, fitted to the
   hours rather than the days) gives `snow` or, with the wind over its threshold, `blizzard`; rain gives `thunder`
   (two-hour episodes, `p_thunder` of the rain hours weighted to the afternoon), `storm` (the wind) or `rain_steady`;
2. **foggy**: calm and the fog over its threshold;
3. the cloud class with hysteresis followed from half a day back: `clear_steady`, `partly`, `cloudy_no_sun`.

What falls: `mm/h = base * exp(1.5 * (q - threshold))`, the weather density that drops it in this phase
(`(mm/h / (25 rain or 2.5 snow))^(2/3)`), eased in and out over 20 minutes at the edge of a wet block next to a dry one.
The air: the month's mean, the anomaly, the day's swing (wider under a clear sky), held consistent with what falls
(no snow at +8 C). The wind: the month's mean, log-normal (`wind_sigma`), at least `blizzard_ms` in a blizzard or a
rainstorm.

Calibrated against 20 simulated years (the Python twin of the model): precipitation per month to the Chornobyl
normals, snow Jan 7.6 / Feb 10.2 cm mean (Kyiv 9 / 11), pond ice 16 cm on 1 Jan and 23 cm on 1 Feb (the research's
Stefan estimate 18 / 27), ice that carries a man (7 cm) on 79 % of December's days, 97 % of January's, 50 % of
March's, 10 % of November's.

## The sky the player sees

`level_weathers.WeatherManager` (AtmosFear) keeps its job - night brightness, moon phases, blowouts, the depth of
field - and asks the climate which sky to set: `af3_<night brightness>_<sky>[_<moon>]`. A new sky lerps in over the
cycle's next key (forced only on a level's first selection and after a jump of the clock). The rest of the scripts
know a climate sky by the stock state it maps on (`snow` -> cloudy, `blizzard`/`thunder` -> storm, `partly` -> clear).
AtmosFear's distant storms only rumble under the climate's thunder.

Dead Air's stock cycles tell a day's story (a sunny noon in the rain cycle, a 200 m fog at noon in the cloudy one), so
the climate's skies are cycles that hold one sky all day, built from Dead Air's own keys:

| Cycle | From | Change |
|---|---|---|
| `clear_steady` | `af3_day_clear` + each moon's night | the afternoon dimming (14:00-16:00) bridged from 13:00 to 17:00 |
| `rain_steady` | `af3_day_storm`, `af3_*_storm` | fog 2.5x farther, sky light 1.2x, rain ambients, no lightning |
| `snow` | `af3_day_cloudy_no_sun`, `af3_*_cloudy_no_sun` | the air luminous grey-white, a cooler sky light, fog ambients |
| `blizzard` | `af3_day_storm`, `af3_*_storm` | a white-grey wall at 150 m, the gale's ambients |

`partly`, `cloudy_no_sun`, `foggy`, `storm` and `thunder` are Dead Air's own. Whatever the cycle draws, the climate's
precipitation (its density and wetness), its phase and its wind replace the cycle's while the option is on (a weather
effect - a blowout, a psi storm - keeps its own). The engine's precipitation fog does the rest: a heavy snowfall
closes the view to some 600 m, and blowing snow adds `10 drift^2` per km - a blizzard is a couple of hundred metres.

**Design fog.** A level whose two AtmosFear periods are both `veryfoggy` keeps its own weather in every season: that
is how Dead Air designs its fog levels (Rostok, the Red Forest). What falls there is still the climate's.

## Day and night

The season sets the length of the day (`r__season_daylight`, on by default). The sun stands where it stands over
Chornobyl (51.27 N, 30.22 E) on the season day at the level's clock in Kyiv civil time, summer time from late March
to late October: up 7:55-16:15 in mid-January, 4:45-21:15 at midsummer. The weather cycles were drawn for a day lit
from 6:15 to 19:55; the **sky clock** maps the level's clock onto it - sunrise onto 6:15, solar noon onto its middle,
sunset onto 19:55, the hour and a half of twilight either side kept at its own length, the night stretched or
squeezed - so a cycle's dawn and dusk fall on the season's. `CEnvironment::GetGameTime()` is that clock (the cycles,
the ambients, frost and dew, the AI's light); the level's own is `GetLevelTime()`. A weather effect holds the offset;
afterwards, or when the season jumps, it eases over at six sky seconds a second. Lua: `level.sky_hours()`. The stock
"is it day" helpers (`xr_conditions.is_day/is_night/is_dark_night`, `utils.is_day`, the level lights' and
helicopters' `is_night`) and the NPCs' torches (`sr_light`) ask the sky's hour; quest timings keep the clock.

## Snow

The snowpack of open ground (`SDaClimateState`): water equivalent, density, the loose top of the last fall.

- **Accumulation** only from what falls: snow and half of sleet, at fresh density 50-200 kg/m3 by the air's
  temperature (Hedstrom and Pomeroy), packed denser by a wind over 6 m/s.
- **Melt**: degree days on the day's mean (`ddf`, low under December's sun) and the rain's warmth by the hour.
- **Settling** towards 260 kg/m3 dry, 450 wet; the loose top settles into the pack over a day and melts first.
- **Blowing snow** (`drift`): a wind over 6 m/s lifting loose dry snow, full in a blizzard.

The picture: the level's snow LUT (WINTER.md) of the season day whose open mean depth the pack has - on the way in
(1 Nov - 15 Feb) a winter that has come that far, on the way out (15 Feb - 15 May) a spring that melted that far, past
the deepest key the same picture deeper - with the fresh fall laid over it (S += fresh, the bare patches whitened by
`exp(-fresh / 2 cm)`). Quantised to 2 mm, so the composites rerun only when the picture moves by a step they can
store. The trails refill from the fall and from what the wind carries (4 cm an hour at full drift). Without snowfall
nothing grows; a thaw rains or melts it away; the grass shows through what the pack leaves.

Blowing snow is also seen: low, nearly level streams of fine grains round knee to chest height (`Rain.cpp`,
`da_rain::drift_*`), under a blizzard's snowfall and on their own in a ground blizzard.

## Ice

The water's ice (`SDaClimateState::ice`, standing [0] and running [1] water): the water follows the air (4 and 9 days)
until it skins over at 0 C; then Stefan's law, `h^2 += alpha^2 FDD` with alpha 2.4 cm per sqrt(C day) on bare ice,
slowed by the snow on it, and a degree-day thaw (0.6 cm per C day, more under an open sky); running water grows its ice
slower and loses it faster. A thaw rots the spring ice (`ice_rot`, C days since it last grew): rotten ice carries half
its thickness. The cover closes over at 3 cm (standing) and 12 cm (running: a lane over the current until then): the front grows out from
the banks (da_snow_ice.h's rule) and thins to nothing across its outer half. The snow on the ice is what fell once it
held 4 cm, swept by the wind.

**Under a weight** (`[ice]` of `dead_air_x64_water.ltx`, the Ukrainian emergency service's rule):

| Ice where the man stands | What happens |
|---|---|
| none / open water / a hole | the water as always (the mesh is passable, the bed carries him) |
| under 3 cm (`hold`) | nothing carries him: he goes through and breaks a hole |
| 3-7 cm | it carries him while it cracks, louder and faster the longer he stands and the faster he moves; then it gives way under him (0.25 s on 3 cm, 4 s near 7 cm; a landing at once) |
| 7 cm and more (`safe`) | it carries him; ice under twice that still creaks now and then |

The physics: the water's triangles stay passable, and a contact on one is a contact on the runtime material
`materials\ice` (a copy of `materials\flooring_tile` with its own steps, hits and particles) where the ice holds and
the body lies on the surface (no more than 25 cm into it): the actor, corpses, items and grenades lie on the ice; a
body already standing through it (wading in a hole, on the bed) passes. A man in the water under the ice's plane
breaks a channel as he goes. The level's AI graph never changes: its creatures keep off the water as always.

**Holes** (`da_ice_hole_add`, at most 96): a hole of open water (a man, a blast) and a bullet's star of cracks. The
picture draws the 16 nearest the camera (`da_ice_holes`: a ragged edge of broken plates, radial cracks and a ring round
a hole, a white bruise and a star round a bullet's mark); inside a hole the water is water (rings, splashes, waves). In
frost a hole skins over in `refreeze_hours`; a load forgets them.

**Bullets**: on ice that holds, the bullet stops on (or skips off) the ice material - chips, its crack, a star; through
ice too thin to stop it, a small hole, a splash and a star, no ring; into open water, the ring and the splash as ever.
**Blasts** break a hole by the blast's radius and the ice's strength (thick winter ice only cracks round a small one).
**Sounds** (synthesized, `tools/ice/ice_sounds.py`): boots on ice, objects knocked on it, bullets on thick and thin
ice, the cracking (crackle and the falling whistle of a struck sheet), the break (the crash, the plates, the plunge).

The picture of the ice thickens with the climate: young ice is black and shows the water through it, a hand's width
does not; rotten spring ice is grey-white and porous.

## QA

| Command | Does |
|---|---|
| `qa_climate_state` | the weather now (sky, air, cloud, what falls, wind, fog), the snowpack, the ice, the thresholds, the next 24 hours of skies |
| `qa_climate_force <sky\|off> [precip] [air]` | forces the live weather (the history keeps the climate's own) |
| `qa_snowpack <m> [fresh m]` | sets the snowpack (the next load recomputes it) |
| `qa_ice <m> [running m]` | sets the ice |

Known limits: the snow on the ice does not take prints (the trails live in the ground's snow); the climate is one for
the whole Zone (no level runs warmer or colder).
